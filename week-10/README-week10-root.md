## The Big Ideas (Explained Simply)

- **Security goals (CIA):** keep private things private (**confidentiality**), keep information correct and untampered (**integrity**), and keep systems working when people need them (**availability**).
- **Business assets:** the things a business depends on — accounts, applications, laptops, websites, backups. Security starts by asking "what needs protecting, and why does the business need it?"
- **Threat / event:** something that could happen (a stolen laptop, a fake login email).
- **Vulnerability:** the weakness that lets the event cause harm (no screen lock, a shared password).
- **Consequence:** what actually goes wrong for the business or people if it happens.
- **Evidence vs assumptions:** write down what you *saw* in the case, separate from what you *guess*. If you did not see a safeguard, it is **unknown** — not proof it is missing.
- **Likelihood and impact:** how likely the event is and how bad it would be, each rated 1–3. Multiplying them gives a classroom risk score that helps you decide what to fix first.
- **Controls and residual risk:** a control is a practical action that reduces risk. **Residual risk** is what is left afterwards. No control makes risk zero.

## The Two Required Labs (In Order)

1. **Lab 1 — Investigate What Needs Protection** (Lessons 1–4). Walk all five clinic rooms, collect evidence, and explain five scenarios.
2. **Lab 2 — Prioritize Risks and Recommend Controls** (Lessons 5–6). **Requires Lab 1.** Rate your five scenarios, choose two priorities, recommend controls, and write a short briefing.
